Why the Pomodoro Technique Still Works (25 Years Later)

Francesco Cirillo invented the Pomodoro Technique in the late 1980s using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. Decades of productivity research have followed — and the core insight has held up: the human brain isn't built for marathon focus. It thrives on rhythm. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Bingo Break free to use?

Yes, completely. There are no accounts, no subscriptions, and no ads. Your settings and scores are saved locally in your browser — nothing is sent to a server.

Can I customize the break activities?

Absolutely. Tap the ✏ Edit button on the Bingo card to replace any of the 16 default activities with whatever fits your life — a specific stretch, a breathing exercise, a line of a book.

What's the difference between 25/5 and 50/10 mode?

The classic Pomodoro is 25 minutes of work followed by a 5-minute break. The 50/10 variant suits people who find they need more runway to reach deep focus — the longer session rewards sustained attention, and the 10-minute break gives you enough time for something more restorative.

How does the scoring system work?

You earn points each time you mark a bingo activity complete. Completing the same activity multiple times in a day earns escalating multipliers. Completing a full row, column, or diagonal earns a line bonus, and clearing the entire board earns a blackout bonus.

Will my progress reset if I close the browser?

No. Everything is saved to your browser's local storage as you go. Your scores, cell completions, and settings persist between sessions. Daily scores reset at midnight.

Can I use Bingo Break on my phone?

Yes — the card deck is fully swipeable on touch devices. You can swipe left and right between cards just as you would on mobile, and the layout adapts to smaller screens.
